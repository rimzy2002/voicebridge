Academic & Native-Level Writing

The Elements of Style — William Strunk Jr. & E. B. White (Written for native speakers. You need a high baseline fluency to actually understand and apply these stylistic rules).

Academic Writing: A Handbook for International Students — Stephen Bailey (Academic writing demands high-level vocabulary and complex structural proficiency).

Longman Academic Writing Series — Alice Oshima & Ann Hogue (Target the highest volumes in this series, usually levels 4 and 5, to get advanced utility out of it).

Expert Grammar & Structure

Grammar for English Language Teachers — Martin Parrott (Designed for instructors. This forces you to understand the underlying mechanics of the language, not just memorize basic rules).

How English Works — Michael Swan & Catherine Walter (Pushes beyond basic grammar into complex, upper-level practical usage).

The Bridge Book

English Vocabulary in Use: Upper-intermediate — Michael McCarthy & Felicity O'Dell (The hardest remaining book on your list. Use this to patch up any remaining gaps before tackling the C1/C2 material)


The New Advanced Additions

Advanced Grammar in Use — Martin Hewings (The gold standard for C1/C2 level practice. If you master this, your grammar is virtually flawless).

Oxford Practice Grammar: Advanced — George Yule (Excellent for diagnosing exactly where your advanced grammar is still failing through rigorous exercises).

Practical English Usage — Michael Swan (This isn't a textbook to read front-to-back; it's a diagnostic reference bible. When you encounter a weird exception or a subtle nuance you don't understand, look it up here).

Understanding and Using English Grammar — Betty Schrampfer Azar & Stacy Hagen (The blue book. This is the highest level in the Azar series and bridges the gap between upper-intermediate and advanced sentence structure).
