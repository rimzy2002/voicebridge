# WEEK 3 — COMMUNICATE PROFESSIONALLY

## DAYS 15–21 ENTERPRISE IMPLEMENTATION PROMPT

Continue from Days 1–14.

Do not create a separate “business English product.”

Use the same curriculum engine with track-specific scenarios.

Week 3 should increase real-world pressure while preserving the same core communication principles:

* clear > complicated;
* natural > memorized;
* communication > perfection;
* speak → feedback → retry;
* evidence-based personalization;
* intelligibility rather than accent removal.

---

## 3. Professional Communication Baseline

Prompt:

“Explain what you do, study, or spend most of your time working on.”

60 sec.

## 9. Networking Roleplay

## 10. Exit a Conversation

* I won't keep you, but it was nice meeting you.

# DAY 16 — PARTICIPATE IN MEETINGS

## 4. Contributing an Idea

## 8. Clarification

Recycle:

## 9. Meeting Question Quality

# DAY 17 — JOB INTERVIEWS AND HIGH-STAKES QUESTIONS

## Title

**Day 17 — Answer Interview Questions with Structure and Evidence**

## Objective

Develop clear interview responses without memorized robotic scripts.

---

## 1. Interview Confidence

1–10.

Ask hardest area:

* introduce myself;
* experience;
* strengths;
* weaknesses;
* examples;
* unexpected questions;
* vocabulary;
* nervousness;
* concise answers.

---

## 2. Interview Baseline

Questions:

“Tell me about yourself.”

Then:

“Tell me about a challenge you handled.”

Record.

---

## 3. Tell Me About Yourself

Teach compact structure:

### PRESENT

What do you do now?

### RELEVANT PAST

What experience brought you here?

### VALUE / STRENGTH

What are you good at?

### DIRECTION

What are you looking for?

Avoid autobiography.

---

## 4. STAR

Reuse existing STAR framework:

Situation.

Task.

Action.

Result.

Do not recreate a second STAR implementation.

---

## 5. STAR Builder

Use learner's real experience where they choose.

Do not invent achievements for them.

---

## 6. Strong vs Weak Evidence

Weak:

“I am good at teamwork.”

Better:

brief evidence showing teamwork.

Teach proof, not adjectives.

---

## 7. Strength Question

Require:

strength + evidence + relevance.

---

## 8. Weakness Question

Avoid fake weaknesses.

Teach:

real manageable area + action taken + improvement.

Do not coach deception.

---

## 9. Behavioral Questions

Examples:

* difficult problem;
* disagreement;
* deadline;
* leadership;
* mistake;
* teamwork;
* learning quickly;
* customer/student challenge.

---

## 10. Unexpected Questions

Use impromptu engine.

Learner has a few seconds to organize.

---

## 11. Follow-Up Interviewing

AI asks follow-ups based on learner's actual answer.

Do not use static disconnected question list only.

---

## 12. Progressive Difficulty

Round 1:
supportive interviewer.

Round 2:
neutral interviewer.

Round 3:
challenging interviewer.

---

## 13. Main Interview Simulation

Approximately 8–10 minutes.

Score:

* relevance;
* structure;
* evidence;
* concision;
* STAR;
* vocabulary;
* clarity;
* recovery;
* confidence behavior.

## Title

**Day 18 — Present Information and Explain Data Clearly**

## Objective

---

## 1. Recall

---

## 2. Presentation Opening

Practice three openings:

* formal;
* neutral;
* conversational.

---

## 3. Presentation Roadmap

Teach concise signposting.

---

## 4. Data Narration

Use uploaded Data Narration feature.

Where chart support exists, display accessible chart plus equivalent data table/text.

Teach vocabulary such as:

* increase;
* rise;
* climb;
* surge where truly strong;
* decline;
* fall;
* drop;
* fluctuate;
* remain stable;
* plateau;
* peak;
* reach a low;
* gradually;
* sharply;
* slightly.

Do not force learners to avoid ordinary “up/down” language if it is clearest.

## 5. Trend → Evidence → Meaning

Teach:

### WHAT?

What happened?

### EVIDENCE

What numbers/details matter?

### INTERPRETATION

Why might it matter?

Do not allow AI to invent causal explanations from a graph.

Learner may say:

“One possible explanation is...”

when inference is not proven.

---

## 6. Graph Description

---

## 7. Compare Two Trends

## 8. Explain Without Visual

---

## 9. Audience Adaptation

Explain same data to:

* manager/professor;
* colleague/classmate;
* general audience.

---

## 10. Presentation Delivery

Recycle:

chunking;
pauses;
intonation;
signposting.

---

## 11. Questions

AI asks:

* clarification;
* reason;
* challenge.

Learner handles Q&A.

---

## 12. Difficult Question Buffer

Introduce professional buffer phrases such as:

* That's a fair question.
* Let me clarify that.
* Based on the information we have...
* I don't have that figure with me, but...
* That's something we'd need to examine further.

Never encourage fabrication.

If learner does not know:

say so professionally.

---

## 13. Main Presentation

3–5 minutes.

Optional chart/data.

---

## 14. Score

* structure;
* signposting;
