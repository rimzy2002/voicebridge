**Question → Thought → English**

# 4. Teach the 3-second rule — 2 minutes

Introduce a simple habit.

# The 3-Second Rule

When someone asks you a question:

**Begin your answer within 3 seconds.**

Instead of waiting for a perfect sentence, use a starting phrase.

Teach five starter phrases:

* I'd say...
* I think...
* In my case...
* From my experience...
* One thing that comes to mind is...

Example:

Question:

> What's the best way to improve communication?

Instead of waiting:

> Umm...mmm...I don't know...

Start:

> I'd say regular speaking practice is probably the best way...

The learner now has a bridge into the answer.

# 6. Sentence-building lesson — 4 minutes

Now teach learners how to grow ideas.

Start with:

> I like working from home.

Then teach:

### Level 1 — Basic

> I like working from home.

### Level 2 — Add reason

> I like working from home because I can concentrate better.

### Level 3 — Add detail

> I like working from home because I can concentrate better, especially when I'm doing work that requires focus.

### Level 4 — Add example

> For example, when I'm preparing reports, I usually finish them faster at home.

### Level 5 — Add contrast

> However, I still prefer going to the office when I need to collaborate with my team.

Now the learner sees that advanced communication often comes from **developing simple ideas**, not using extremely difficult words.

# 8. Day 2 communication framework — IDEA

Day 1 used **PREP**.

Day 2 can introduce another simple tool:

# IDEA

**I — Idea**
What do you think?

**D — Detail**
Explain more.

**E — Example**
Give an example.

**A — Add something else**
Contrast, result, experience, or conclusion.

Example:

> Learning English is important.

**Idea:**
English is important for professional development.

**Detail:**
Many international companies use English for communication.

**Example:**
For example, employees may need English during meetings with overseas clients.

**Add:**
It can also provide access to more career opportunities.

IDEA helps learners extend answers naturally.

# 10. What to do when you forget a word — 4 minutes

This is extremely important.

Teach:

# Don't Stop — Describe It

Suppose the learner forgets the word:

**“umbrella.”**

Instead of stopping:

> Yesterday I forgot my...mmm...mmm...

They can say:

> Yesterday I forgot the thing we use when it rains.

The listener understands.

Teach three techniques.

### Technique 1 — Describe its purpose

> It's something we use to...

### Technique 2 — Describe its appearance

> It looks like...

### Technique 3 — Give the category

> It's a type of...

Examples:

**Calculator**

> It's something we use to calculate numbers.

**Receptionist**

> It's a person who usually welcomes visitors at an office or hotel.

**Headphones**

> They're something you wear on your ears to listen to audio.

This skill dramatically improves fluency.

# 15. Filler-word awareness — 3 minutes

Introduce one fluency issue:

**umm / ah / like / you know / actually**

Explain:

Fillers aren't always bad.

But too many make speech sound uncertain.

Teach:

### Replace filler words with a short pause.

Instead of:

> I think...umm...technology is...umm...important because...

Use:

> I think... technology is important because...

A silent **1-second pause** can sound confident.

# What Day 2 should achieve

By the end of Day 2, the learner should understand:

**1. I don't need perfect English to communicate.**

**2. I can start answering faster.**

**3. I know how to extend a simple sentence.**

**4. I can describe a word when I don't know its exact name.**

**5. I should pause instead of filling every silence with “umm.”**

**6. I can speak continuously for around 1–2 minutes.**

**7. Thinking directly in English is a trainable skill.**

That gives us a clean progression:

**Day 1 — Know your level + structure your ideas**

↓

**Day 2 — Think faster + keep speaking**

↓

**Day 3 — Build longer, richer conversations**

This progression is important. Each day should feel like the learner is adding **one communication superpower**, rather than completing another random English lesson.
