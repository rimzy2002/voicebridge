For **Day 6**, the system should focus on **pronunciation for clarity**, not accent removal. The goal is to help learners sound easier to understand, more confident, and more natural.

# Day 6 — Speak Clearly: Pronunciation, Stress and Rhythm

**Main objective:** Improve clarity by training sentence stress, word stress, rhythm, pausing, intonation, and a small number of high-impact pronunciation issues.

**Target outcome:** By the end of Day 6, the learner should be able to deliver a **2-minute response with clearer stress, better pacing, fewer pronunciation breakdowns, and more natural intonation**.

**Recommended duration:** 35–45 minutes
**Quick version:** 12–15 minutes

Core lesson:

> **You do not need a different accent. You need speech that is easy to understand.**

# 10. Sentence Stress Lesson — 4 minutes

Now teach that not every word receives equal emphasis.

Example:

> I **NEED** to finish the **REPORT** by **FRIDAY**.

The important information receives more stress.

Less important grammar words are usually lighter.

Compare robotic:

> I. NEED. TO. FINISH. THE. REPORT. BY. FRIDAY.

Natural:

> I **NEED** to finish the **REPORT** by **FRIDAY**.

Explain:

### Stress the meaning words

Usually:

* Important nouns
* Main verbs
* Adjectives
* Adverbs
* Important numbers/names

# 12. Meaning Changes With Stress — 3 minutes

Show:

> **I** didn't say he cancelled the meeting.

Meaning: someone else said it.

> I didn't **SAY** he cancelled the meeting.

Meaning: perhaps I implied it.

> I didn't say he cancelled the **MEETING**.

Meaning: maybe he cancelled something else.

Teach:

> Stress can change what the listener understands as important.

This is useful for advanced communication.

# 13. Rhythm Training — 4 minutes

Teach learners not to give every word equal timing.

Example:

> I **WANT** to **TALK** about the **PROJECT**.

Instead of:

> I / want / to / talk / about / the / project.

Explain:

English often moves from one stressed beat to another.

Practice:

> We **NEED** to **MAKE** a **DECISION**.

> I'll **SEND** you the **REPORT** tomorrow.

> She **FINISHED** the **PRESENTATION** yesterday.

Learner:

🎧 Listen

↓

👏 Optional tap the stressed beats

↓

🎙️ Repeat

This could be a fun visual exercise.

---

# 15. Chunking Lesson — 3 minutes

Teach learners to speak in meaningful groups.

Poor:

> Yesterday I went to the office and after that I met my manager and then we discussed the project...

Better:

> Yesterday / I went to the office / and after that / I met my manager / to discuss the project.

Introduce:

# Thought Chunks

Instead of reading word by word, group meaning together.

---

# 17. Pause Training — 3 minutes

Connect to previous days.

Teach three kinds of useful pauses:

### 1. Thinking pause

> From my perspective... communication is essential.

### 2. Structure pause

> There are three main reasons... First...

### 3. Emphasis pause

> The biggest problem was... nobody had checked the data.

Explain:

A pause can make speech sound confident.

Too many fillers make it sound less controlled.

# 19. Intonation Lesson — 4 minutes

Teach basic communication intonation.

### Statements

Usually falling:

> I finished the report. ↘

### Yes/no questions

Often rising:

> Did you finish the report? ↗

### Wh-questions

Often falling:

> When did you finish it? ↘

### Surprise

> Really? ↗

### Interest

> That's interesting! ↗↘

Don't overcomplicate this.

The goal is to stop learners sounding completely flat.

# 25. Final Sound Awareness

A common clarity issue is dropping word endings.

Examples:

> work / worked

> ask / asked

> need / needs

> project / projects

Teach:

Final sounds can carry grammar and meaning.

Compare:

> He work every day. ❌

> He works every day. ✅

Learner practices a few words in sentences.

# 27. Connected Speech Awareness — 3 minutes

Words often connect in natural speech.

We should teach **understanding and smoothness**, not force learners into exaggerated contractions.

# 51. Smart Pronunciation Feedback Rules

Our AI should avoid overwhelming users.

If the learner makes 20 pronunciation differences, don't display 20.

Prioritize:

**1. Problems affecting understanding**

**2. Repeated problems**

**3. Problems connected to today's lesson**

Ignore harmless accent differences.

Feedback should be:

> “Make the final /s/ in ‘clients’ clearer.”

Not:

> “Your pronunciation is wrong.”

## What Day 6 should achieve

By the end of Day 6, the learner should feel:

**1. “I don't need to lose my accent to communicate well.”**

**2. “I know which words in a sentence should receive emphasis.”**

**3. “I can divide long sentences into meaningful chunks.”**

**4. “I can pause instead of filling silence with ‘umm’.”**

**5. “My speech doesn't need to be fast to sound fluent.”**

**6. “I can use intonation to sound interested, confident, surprised, or concerned.”**

**7. “I know my personal pronunciation priorities.”**

The curriculum now has a balanced progression:

**Day 1 — Structure thoughts**
**Day 2 — Think faster**
**Day 3 — Maintain conversations**
**Day 4 — Tell stories**
**Day 5 — Upgrade vocabulary**
**Day 6 — Improve clarity and pronunciation**

For **Day 7**, I would make the first major **Weekly Communication Challenge + Review Day**. Instead of introducing many new concepts, Day 7 can test everything from Days 1–6 through a realistic conversation, story, listening task, vocabulary test, pronunciation challenge, and a personalized **Week 1 Progress Report**.
