# Day 4 — Tell Better Stories and Explain Past Experiences

**Main objective:** Help learners talk naturally about things that happened in the past, organize events in sequence, add useful details, and make stories interesting rather than confusing.

**Target outcome:** By the end of Day 4, the learner should be able to tell a clear **2–3 minute story** and explain a past experience using a beginning, middle, and ending.

**Recommended duration:** 35–45 minutes
**Quick version:** 12–15 minutes

The core lesson is:

> **A good story is not about using difficult English. It is about helping the listener understand what happened, why it mattered, and how you felt.**

# 5. Show the Problem

Present two versions.

### Version A

> Yesterday I went outside. I met my friend. Then we ate. Then we went somewhere. It was good. Then I came home.

Ask:

### Is this understandable?

Yes.

### Is it interesting?

Not really.

Now show:

### Version B

> Yesterday evening, I met an old friend whom I hadn't seen for almost a year. We originally planned to have a quick coffee, but we ended up talking for nearly three hours. The funniest part was realizing that we had both recently started working on very similar projects.

Explain:

Both stories communicate the event.

The second story gives the listener:

**Context + sequence + detail + emotion + outcome**

# 6. Teach the STORY Framework

Introduce Day 4's main framework:

# STORY

### S — Set the scene

Where were you?
When did it happen?
Who was there?

### T — Tell what happened

What started the event?

### O — Obstacles / interesting moment

What unexpected, difficult, funny, or important thing happened?

### R — Result

What happened in the end?

### Y — Your reaction

How did you feel?
What did you learn?

This framework can be reused throughout the entire 30-day program.

---

# 7. STORY Example

Prompt:

> Tell me about a difficult day at work.

### S — Set the scene

> Last month, I was preparing an important presentation for a client.

### T — Tell what happened

> About an hour before the meeting, my laptop suddenly stopped working.

### O — Obstacle

> The problem was that my presentation and notes were all saved on that laptop.

### R — Result

> Fortunately, one of my colleagues helped me recover the files, and we joined the meeting only a few minutes late.

### Y — Your reaction

> I was extremely stressed at the time, but the experience taught me to keep important files backed up.

Now ask learner:

> Which part makes the story interesting?

The obstacle.

# 8. Story Builder Activity — 4 minutes

Give a simple event:

> I missed the bus.

Build it step by step.

### Step 1 — Set the scene

> When did this happen?

Learner speaks.

### Step 2 — Tell what happened

> Why did you miss it?

### Step 3 — Add the problem

> What happened because you missed it?

### Step 4 — Result

> What did you do?

### Step 5 — Reaction

> How did you feel?

The system combines the sections into one story.

---

# 9. Story Order Game — 3 minutes

Show events in the wrong order.

Example:

A. I finally reached the airport.

B. I realized I had left my passport at home.

C. Last year, I was traveling to Singapore.

D. My brother brought the passport to me.

E. I was extremely relieved.

Ask learner to arrange:

**C → B → D → A → E**

This teaches sequence before grammar.

---

# 10. Sequencing Words Lesson — 4 minutes

Teach useful storytelling connectors.

### Beginning

* Last week...
* A few months ago...
* One day...
* Recently...
* When I was...

### Continuing

* First...
* Then...
* After that...
* A little later...
* Meanwhile...

### Unexpected event

* Suddenly...
* Surprisingly...
* Unfortunately...
* To my surprise...

### Ending

* Eventually...
* In the end...
* Finally...
* Fortunately...

Avoid giving 30 expressions.

Start with 8–10 high-value ones.

---

# 11. Sequence Challenge — 3 minutes

Give learner five events:

> woke up late
> missed the bus
> called a taxi
> arrived at work
> manager was waiting

Ask them to tell the story using at least four sequencing expressions.

Example:

> Yesterday, I woke up late. First, I rushed to get ready, but unfortunately I missed my bus. After that, I called a taxi. Eventually, I arrived at work, but my manager was already waiting for me.

System detects sequencing language.

# 12. Past-Tense Micro Lesson — 4 minutes

Day 4 can include grammar, but only grammar that directly improves communication.

Focus on:

## Simple Past

> I went.
> I called.
> I realized.
> I forgot.

## Past Continuous

Used for background actions:

> I was driving home when my phone rang.

## Past Perfect — optional advanced layer

Used when one past event happened before another:

> I realized that I had forgotten my wallet.

Do not turn this into a long grammar lesson.

Teach through stories.

# 13. Choose the Right Past Form — 3 minutes

Example:

> I ___ home when I saw an accident.

Options:

* drove
* was driving ✅
* have driven

Then:

> When I arrived, the meeting ___ already ___.

Answer:

> had already started

Then immediately make the learner speak.

Prompt:

> Tell me what you were doing yesterday at 7 p.m.

This activates grammar.

# 14. “What Happened Next?” Game — 3 minutes

AI starts:

> Yesterday evening, I was walking home when I noticed something strange near the road...

Learner continues the story.

After 20–30 seconds, AI says:

> What happened next?

Learner continues.

Then:

> And then?

Continue for approximately 90 seconds.

Purpose:

Teach spontaneous storytelling without planning every sentence.

---

# 15. Add Useful Details — 3 minutes

Teach:

Stories need details, but **not every detail**.

Bad:

> It was Monday. I woke up at 6:42. I brushed my teeth. I used the blue toothbrush...

Too much.

Better details answer:

* Where?
* Who?
* What was unusual?
* What mattered?
* How did you feel?

Give learner:

> I had an interview.

Ask them to add **3 useful details**.

For example:

> It was my first interview for an international company. There were three interviewers, and the interview lasted nearly an hour.

---

# 16. Detail Detector Game

Show:

> Last weekend I went somewhere with someone and we did something. It was nice.

Ask:

### What's missing?

* Place
* Person
* Activity
* Emotion
* Interesting moment

Learner improves the story.

Possible:

> Last weekend, I went hiking with two friends in the mountains. We started early in the morning, and halfway through the hike it suddenly began raining. We were completely unprepared, but we ended up laughing about it.

---

# 17. Emotion Vocabulary — 4 minutes

Instead of always:

> I was happy.

> I was sad.

Teach more useful emotional vocabulary.

### Happy

* excited
* delighted
* relieved
* proud
* grateful

### Negative

* nervous
* frustrated
* embarrassed
* disappointed
* overwhelmed

### Surprise

* surprised
* shocked
* confused
* amazed

Teach differences briefly.

For example:

**Nervous:** worried before something happens.

**Relieved:** happy because a problem is over.

---

# 18. Emotion Upgrade Game — 2 minutes

Replace:

> I was very happy.

Possible:

> I was delighted.

Replace:

> I was very worried.

Possible:

> I was extremely nervous.

Replace:

> I was happy because the problem ended.

Better:

> I was relieved.

Then make learner use two emotions in their own story.

---

# 19. Show, Don't Only Tell — 3 minutes

Teach the difference.

Basic:

> I was nervous.

Better:

> I was so nervous that I checked my presentation three times before the meeting.

Basic:

> I was tired.

Better:

> By the time I got home, I could barely keep my eyes open.

Don't require literary storytelling.

Just make descriptions more natural.

# 20. Today's Five Power Expressions

Choose storytelling phrases.

### 1. It all started when...

> It all started when I received an unexpected phone call.

### 2. The funny thing was...

> The funny thing was that neither of us knew where we were going.

### 3. To my surprise...

> To my surprise, they offered me the job.

### 4. At that point...

> At that point, I realized I had made a mistake.

### 5. In the end...

> In the end, everything worked out well.

These become part of active vocabulary.

# 24. Listening Activity — 4 minutes

Play a 45–60 second story.

Then ask:

### Who was involved?

### Where did it happen?

### What was the problem?

### What happened in the end?

### How did the speaker feel?

Then:

> Retell the story in your own words.

This is important.

Learners should not repeat exactly.

They should **reconstruct meaning**.

# 25. Retelling Challenge — 3 minutes

After listening, hide the transcript.

Show only five keywords.

For example:

**train — interview — delay — phone call — successful**

Learner retells.

* Main information retained
* Sequence
* Paraphrasing
* Fluency

This trains both listening and speaking.

# 26. Pronunciation Focus — Sentence Chunking

Teach learners not to speak as one long stream.

Example:

> Last Friday / I was driving home from work / when suddenly / my car started making a strange noise.

Explain:

Native/natural speech is organized into **thought groups**.

Practice:

> A few weeks ago / I had an interview / for a job I really wanted.

> To my surprise / the interview was much more relaxed / than I expected.

Learner repeats.

---

# 27. Pause for Effect

Day 2 taught:

**Pause instead of “umm.”**

Day 4 expands this.

Good storyteller:

> When I opened the email... **pause** ...I couldn't believe what I was reading.

Teach intentional pause.

Not:

> ummmmmmmmm...

But:

> At that point... I realized I'd forgotten my passport.

This improves naturalness.

---

# 28. Story Repair Skill

Learners sometimes make mistakes while telling stories.

Teach natural recovery phrases:

* Sorry, what I mean is...
* Let me correct that...
* Actually, it happened on Friday, not Thursday.
* I should say...
* Let me put that another way...

The goal:

**Correct quickly and continue.**

Do not restart the entire story.

---

# 29. Story Repair Challenge — 2 minutes

Ask learner to tell a short story.

During it, AI can simulate:

> Sorry, did you mean Monday or Tuesday?

Learner clarifies:

> Sorry, I meant Tuesday.

Then continues.

This trains real conversation resilience.

# 30. Professional Track — STAR Storytelling

For professionals, introduce a useful variation:

# STAR

**S — Situation**

**T — Task**

**A — Action**

**R — Result**

This is useful for interviews.

Question:

> Tell me about a time you solved a difficult problem.

Example structure:

### Situation

> Our team was preparing a report for an important client.

### Task

> I was responsible for analyzing the final data.

### Action

> I discovered an error, checked the source, and worked with another team to correct it.

### Result

> We submitted the report on time and avoided sending incorrect information to the client.

This can become a major recurring framework.

# What Day 4 should achieve

At the end of the day, learners should feel:

**1. “I know how to organize a story before I speak.”**

**2. “I can explain past events without getting completely lost.”**

**3. “I can use sequencing phrases instead of repeating ‘then... then... then...’.”**

**4. “I can add useful details without adding unnecessary information.”**

**5. “I can explain how I felt more naturally.”**

**6. “If I make a mistake, I can correct myself and continue.”**

**7. “I can speak for around 2–3 minutes about a past experience.”**

The curriculum now develops naturally:

**Day 1 — Structure your thoughts**

**Day 2 — Think faster and reduce hesitation**

**Day 3 — Build longer conversations**

**Day 4 — Tell clear and engaging stories**

For **Day 5**, I would move into **vocabulary activation and upgrading basic English into more natural, intermediate-to-advanced English**—for example transforming “very good,” “very difficult,” “I think,” “I want,” and repetitive vocabulary into stronger communication without making learners sound unnatural.
